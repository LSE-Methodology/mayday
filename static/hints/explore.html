Phase 1

Explore the Data

~45 minutes — Get to know the database before you model anything.

1

Start with the big picture

Before diving into specific segments, understand the overall funnel. How many impressions are there? What fraction get clicked? Of those clicks, what fraction lead to a purchase? What's the total revenue?

Tables you'll need: impressions, conversions
2

Break it down by user type

The users table has several columns that describe who someone is: loyalty tier, device type, age group, browsing frequency. Try grouping your funnel metrics by these dimensions. Do all groups behave the same way?

Compare CTR and revenue per impression side by side. Are the rankings the same?
3

Look at the ads too

Ads have metadata about creative quality, headline style, product price, and discounts. Some ads might attract lots of clicks. Does that mean they generate lots of revenue? Check.

Try grouping by headline_clickbait_score (high vs low) and creative_quality_score (high vs low). Look at both CTR and revenue.
4

Think about what "conversion" means here

A click is free for the advertiser to count. A conversion means someone actually bought something. How does the conversion rate (given a click) vary across different user groups? Are the users who click the most also the ones who buy the most?

This is the single most important thing to understand before Phase 2.
5

Ask yourself

Before moving on, you should be able to answer these questions with data:

  • If you could only show ads to one user segment, which would generate the most revenue?
  • Is that the same segment with the highest click-through rate?
  • Do high-clickbait ads make more money than high-quality ads?
