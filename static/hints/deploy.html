Phase 4

Deploy & Test Live

~45 minutes — Ship it, watch it, improve it.

1

Upload and check

After uploading, the server validates your model on a sample input and reports back the predicted score and latency. If the upload fails, the error message will tell you what went wrong — usually a feature name mismatch or a missing .predict() method.

Remember to use cloudpickle.dump() when saving your model, not pickle.dump().
2

Read the dashboard

The leaderboard shows several metrics per team. The ranking is by revenue per impression, but the other columns are useful for diagnosing what's happening:

  • CTR — are your chosen ads getting clicked?
  • CVR|Click — when they click, do they buy?
  • Revenue — total revenue so far
  • Avg Latency — how fast is your model?
  • Timeouts — how often did latency cause a penalty?
3

Diagnose and iterate

If your revenue per impression is low, look at the pattern:

  • High CTR, low CVR — your model is good at predicting clicks but those clicks aren't converting. Rethink your target.
  • Low CTR, high CVR — you're finding the right users but not matching them with the right ads. Check your ad features.
  • Many timeouts — your model is too slow. Go back to Phase 3.
You can re-upload at any time. Your metrics reset with each new model.
4

Watch the charts

The revenue-over-time chart shows cumulative revenue. A steeper line means more revenue per unit of traffic. The revenue-per-impression chart shows the running average — it stabilises over time as noise averages out. Early fluctuations are normal.

5

Reflect

After the simulation ends, think about these questions for the discussion:

  • Why did the winning team's approach work?
  • Did any team have high CTR but low revenue? Why might that happen?
  • If you ran this again, what would you do differently?
  • How does this relate to real-world A/B testing and causal inference?
