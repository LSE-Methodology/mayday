Phase 2

Build Your Model

~75 minutes — The model matters, but the target matters more.

1

Choose your target carefully

You'll be scored on revenue per impression. That's not the same as click-through rate. Think about what target variable best aligns with the metric you're actually being measured on.

You can predict clicks, conversions, revenue, or some combination. What are the tradeoffs of each?
2

You're allowed to use more than one model

Nothing says your final scoring function has to be a single model. You could train separate models for different parts of the problem and combine their predictions. Think about what a user's journey looks like: impression → click → purchase → revenue.

Each step in that chain can be modelled. The question is how to combine them into a single score.
3

Don't forget the ad side

Your model receives both user features and ad features. Two impressions to the same user can have very different values depending on which ad is shown. Make sure your model uses the ad metadata — price, discount, creative quality, etc.

Think about it: even if you perfectly predict who will click, you still need to know what they're clicking on to estimate revenue.
4

Start simple, then iterate

A logistic regression that predicts the right thing will beat a gradient-boosted forest that predicts the wrong thing. Get a baseline working end-to-end first (train, wrap, upload), then improve.

You can re-upload your model at any time during Phase 4. Don't spend all your time here perfecting one approach.
5

Evaluate on the right metric

AUC tells you about ranking quality, but it doesn't directly tell you about revenue. Try this: sort your test set by model score, look at the top 10% vs the bottom 90%. How much more revenue per impression does the top bucket generate?

If your model's top-scored impressions don't have higher revenue, something about your target or features needs rethinking.
