The Scenario

You run ad targeting for an e-commerce company

You have a database of 500,000 ad impressions across 10,000 users and 200 ad creatives.

Your job: decide which ads to show to which users to generate the most revenue.

The Data

Four tables, one funnel

users

10,000 users with demographics, device type, loyalty tier, purchase history, browsing frequency

ads

200 ad creatives with category, price, discount, creative quality, headline style

impressions

500,000 times an ad was shown to a user — with page type, position, time of day, and whether they clicked

conversions

Purchases that followed a click — the revenue generated from each sale

Each step narrows. Not every impression leads to a click. Not every click leads to a purchase. Your model navigates this funnel.

Key Terms

Metrics you'll see

CTR — Click-Through Rate

Fraction of impressions that get clicked. clicks / impressions

CVR — Conversion Rate

Fraction of clicks that lead to a purchase. conversions / clicks

Revenue per Impression

Average revenue generated each time an ad is shown. This is what you're scored on.

Latency

How long your model takes to return a prediction. Measured in milliseconds.

Key Concept

What is an A/B test?

A controlled experiment where different strategies are tested on the same traffic at the same time.

In our workshop

  • Each team's model is a different strategy
  • All teams see the same stream of users
  • The simulation decides outcomes using the same rules
  • We compare results on a shared dashboard

In the real world

  • Traffic is randomly split between variants
  • Each variant runs simultaneously
  • Statistical tests determine the winner
  • Used everywhere: ads, product features, pricing
Your Objective

Maximise revenue per impression

Your model receives user features and a set of candidate ads. It scores each ad. The highest-scoring ad gets shown. Then we see what happens.

Think carefully about what to optimise. The metric you're scored on is revenue per impression — not clicks, not accuracy, not AUC.

Under the Hood

How the simulation works

  • A stream of simulated users arrive continuously
  • For each user, the server picks 10 candidate ads
  • Your model scores all 10 — the highest-scored ad is shown
  • A ground-truth model determines if the user clicks and buys
  • Revenue is tracked per team on the live dashboard
Constraint

Latency matters

The simulation has a latency budget. If your model is too slow to score a batch, there's a chance that request gets assigned a random ad instead of your model's choice.

Fast model

Every impression uses your model's prediction. No wasted traffic.

Slow model

Some impressions fall back to random ads. Revenue you could have earned is lost.

This is a real production constraint. Ad systems typically require predictions in 10–50ms.
